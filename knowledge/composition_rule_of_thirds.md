The rule of thirds divides the frame with two horizontal and two vertical lines; placing a subject along these lines or their intersections creates dynamic, off-center compositions that feel active and leave room for the eye to travel. Centered composition, by contrast, confers stillness, formality, symmetry, and authority — it can feel stable or unsettlingly rigid depending on context (Kubrick and Wes Anderson exploit central framing deliberately). Negative space — the empty area around a subject — shapes mood: heavy negative space isolates or diminishes a figure, while tight framing with little negative space creates pressure and confinement. A measured subject position in a side third with substantial empty space is the classic "looking room" composition.
