Compositional lines guide the viewer's eye through the image. Leading lines — roads, corridors, railings, light beams — pull attention toward a focal point and create depth and direction. Framing within the frame (using doorways, windows, mirrors, or foreground objects to enclose the subject) concentrates attention and can imply entrapment, voyeurism, separation, or psychological boundaries. Horizontal lines feel calm and stable; verticals feel formal or imposing; diagonals feel dynamic and energetic. When a composition is described as confined or obstructed, internal framing is often doing the work, shaping how the subject relates to the space around them and what the audience is invited to feel about their situation.
