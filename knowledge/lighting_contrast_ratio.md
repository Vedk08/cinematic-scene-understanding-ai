Contrast ratio describes the difference in brightness between the lit and shadowed sides of a subject. High contrast (hard light, deep shadows, large tonal range) feels dramatic, tense, harsh, or stylized and is central to noir, thriller, and many character-study looks. Low contrast (soft, even illumination, compressed tonal range) feels gentle, naturalistic, nostalgic, or flat depending on context, and suits comedy, romance, and documentary realism. Hard light from a small, distant, or undiffused source creates crisp shadow edges and texture; soft light from a large or diffused source wraps around the subject and minimizes shadow edges. Measured high standard deviation in luminance with a large dark area signals a high-contrast, hard-light aesthetic.
