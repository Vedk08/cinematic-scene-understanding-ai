Horror leans on visual withholding and instability. Low-key lighting with large pools of darkness hides threats and exploits fear of the unseen; negative space and off-center framing leave room for something to appear. Unstable devices — Dutch angles, handheld movement, sudden scale shifts — create unease. Cool or sickly palettes (desaturated greens, cold blues) signal dread, while sudden warm or red accents can mark violence or alarm. Confined framing and internal framing (doorways, mirrors) evoke entrapment and vulnerability. When measurements show heavy shadow, confined or asymmetrical composition, and a cold palette, the image aligns with horror's grammar of concealment and threat, though many films borrow these tools outside the genre.
