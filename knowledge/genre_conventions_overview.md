Genres carry loose visual grammars that audiences read intuitively. Horror leans on low-key lighting, negative space that hides threats, and unstable framing. Romance and comedy favor high-key, warm, soft light and balanced, open compositions. Science fiction often uses cool palettes and clean, geometric framing to signal control or sterility; epics and westerns use wide shots and natural light to foreground landscape and scale. Period dramas use motivated, candle-or-window light and warmer grades. These are conventions, not rules — filmmakers confirm, subvert, or blend them deliberately. A grounded analysis should treat a feature match as evidence for a convention, then note where the image confirms or deliberately departs from the expected grammar.
