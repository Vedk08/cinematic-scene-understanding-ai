Shot size describes how much of the subject fills the frame, and each size does narrative work. A close-up isolates a face or detail, maximizing emotional intimacy and forcing the viewer to read interiority; it removes context and concentrates attention. A medium shot frames a subject roughly waist-up, balancing facial performance with body language and immediate surroundings — the workhorse of dialogue. A wide (or long) shot places the subject within the environment, emphasizing space, scale, geography, and the subject's relationship to their world; it can diminish a character or establish isolation. Moving from wide to close across a scene typically signals rising emotional pressure; the reverse releases it. The size choice is inseparable from blocking and lens.
