Three-point lighting is the foundational setup most narrative scenes are built on. The key light is the primary, strongest source and defines the dominant direction and quality of light on the subject. The fill light is a softer, lower-intensity source placed roughly opposite the key; it lifts the shadows the key creates and controls contrast. A high key-to-fill ratio (weak fill) yields hard, dramatic shadows; a low ratio (strong fill) yields a flat, even, "high-key" look. The back light (or rim/kicker) comes from behind the subject and separates them from the background by tracing a bright edge along hair and shoulders. When an analysis infers a single strong directional key with minimal fill and heavy shadow, it is describing a low-key, high-contrast variant of this setup; even, shadowless illumination implies strong fill and a higher overall exposure.
