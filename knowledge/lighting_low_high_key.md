"Key" here refers to overall tonal level, not the key light. Low-key lighting is dominated by darkness: large areas of shadow, a small ratio of lit to unlit area, and strong contrast. It is conventionally associated with tension, mystery, threat, moral ambiguity, and night interiors, and is a hallmark of film noir, thriller, and horror. High-key lighting is the inverse: bright, low-contrast, shadows filled in, used for comedy, romance, musicals, and "clean" commercial looks. A measured high dark-ratio with strong contrast points toward low-key; a high mean brightness with soft, reduced shadows points toward high-key. Neither is inherently better; the read is about whether the tonal choice supports the scene's intended emotional register.
