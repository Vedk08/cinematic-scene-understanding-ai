Blocking is not only static arrangement but movement through the frame. Characters moving toward the camera grow in presence and intensity; moving away, they diminish or retreat emotionally. Crossing the frame, converging, or separating choreographs relationships and shifts of power in real time. Stillness within an otherwise active frame draws the eye; a single moving figure among static ones commands attention. Even in a still frame, posture and implied motion — leaning in, turning away, reaching — encode the same dynamics. Reading blocking means asking who moves, who holds still, who occupies the strong position, and how those choices track the scene's emotional beats.
