Film noir is defined visually as much as narratively. Its conventions include low-key, high-contrast lighting with deep shadows; hard single-source or motivated practical light (street lamps, venetian-blind slats casting barred shadows); cool or desaturated palettes; unbalanced, claustrophobic compositions with canted angles and foreground obstruction; and night-for-night exteriors. These choices externalize the genre's themes of moral ambiguity, paranoia, entrapment, and fatalism. When an analysis detects low-key dramatic lighting, strong shadow contrast, a cool tone, and asymmetrical or confined composition together, those features align with noir convention — useful as a comparison point even for contemporary films that quote the noir tradition (neo-noir).
