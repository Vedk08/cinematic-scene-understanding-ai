Warm palettes (reds, oranges, ambers) and cool palettes (blues, teals, greens) carry conventional associations that filmmakers exploit. Warm tones commonly read as intimacy, comfort, nostalgia, memory, heat, or safety; cool tones read as isolation, melancholy, detachment, night, clinical control, or unease. A neutral or desaturated palette tends to feel restrained, realistic, or emotionally withheld. The "teal-and-orange" contrast is a widespread modern grade because skin tones (warm) pop against cool backgrounds. When a dominant palette is extracted via clustering, the proportion of warm versus cool mass — not just the presence of a hue — drives the emotional read. A single warm practical light in an otherwise cool frame is a deliberate focal accent.
