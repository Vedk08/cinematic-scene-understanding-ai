The vertical angle of the camera relative to the subject encodes power and psychology. A low-angle shot looks up at a subject, enlarging them to suggest dominance, heroism, threat, or grandeur. A high-angle shot looks down, shrinking the subject to imply vulnerability, weakness, submission, or surveillance. An eye-level angle is neutral and observational, placing the viewer on equal footing with the character. A Dutch (canted) angle tilts the horizon, creating instability, disorientation, tension, or psychological unease — common in thrillers and horror. Angle works together with shot size and blocking: a low-angle close-up of a single dominant subject amplifies their imposing presence, while a high-angle wide shot can make a figure look lost in their environment.
