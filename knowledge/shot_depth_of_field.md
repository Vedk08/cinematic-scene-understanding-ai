Depth of field is the range of distance that appears acceptably sharp. Shallow depth of field isolates a subject against a soft, blurred background (bokeh), directing the eye and creating intimacy, subjectivity, or dreaminess; it is associated with longer lenses and wide apertures. Deep focus keeps foreground and background both sharp, letting the viewer's eye roam and allowing complex staging in depth where multiple planes of action carry meaning simultaneously (a technique famously exploited in classical Hollywood). Rack focus — shifting focus from one plane to another within a shot — redirects attention and can reveal relationships or surprises. A tight close-up with a clearly separated background implies shallow depth and a desire to isolate the subject from context.
