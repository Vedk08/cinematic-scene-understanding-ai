Where a subject sits within the frame, and how much space is left around them, shapes comfort and meaning. Headroom is the gap above the head: too much makes a subject feel small or lost, too little feels cramped or tense. Lead room (or nose room) is space in the direction a subject faces or moves; ample lead room feels balanced and forward-looking, while denying it — placing a subject hard against the edge they face — creates unease, confinement, or a sense of being blocked. These conventions are deliberately broken to unsettle the viewer. A subject pressed to one edge with heavy negative space on the opposite side reads as isolation or imbalance.
