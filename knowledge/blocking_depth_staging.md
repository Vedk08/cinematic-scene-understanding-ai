Staging in depth arranges subjects and action across foreground, middle ground, and background rather than on a single flat plane. A figure placed large in the foreground with another small in the background creates hierarchy, tension, or dramatic irony — the audience reads the relationship through size and position. Depth staging rewards deep focus and makes the frame feel three-dimensional and alive. Overlapping planes (a foreground object partially blocking the subject) add layering and can imply observation, obstruction, or intimacy. When detected subjects differ sharply in scale and sit on different planes, the composition is using depth to encode power, distance, or connection between characters.
