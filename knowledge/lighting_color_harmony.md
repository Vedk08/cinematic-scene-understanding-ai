Beyond warm and cool, the relationship between colors in the frame carries meaning. Complementary schemes (opposites on the color wheel, e.g. orange and blue) create vivid visual tension and separation, which is why teal-and-orange grading is so common for making warm skin pop against cool surroundings. Analogous schemes (neighboring hues) feel harmonious, unified, and calm. A monochromatic palette concentrates mood and can feel stylized, oppressive, or immersive. High color saturation feels heightened, energetic, or surreal; desaturation feels bleak, realistic, harsh, or melancholic. When a palette is dominated by a single hue family, the emotional read intensifies; when two opposed hues split the frame, look for a deliberate contrast between subject and world.
